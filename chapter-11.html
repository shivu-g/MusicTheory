Scales from melodic and harmonic minor

The melodic minor scale has seven modes of its own, and three of them are everyday tools in jazz and fusion. Harmonic minor contributes one more. Each is best learned as "the scale for one kind of chord".

2Core

Altered dominant chords

A dominant 7 chord is already tense. Raising or lowering its 9th, 11th or 13th adds notes that sit a half step from the chord you are resolving to, so the release is stronger. The four alterations are ♭9, ♯9, ♯11 and ♭13; "alt" means some mixture of them.

Dominant chords that resolve elsewhere

A dominant 7 chord usually falls a 5th to its target, but it has other exits. Knowing them tells you what a surprising chord in a tune is doing, and which scale suits it: a dominant heading down a 5th takes Mixolydian or the altered scale; a dominant going anywhere else usually sounds best with Lydian dominant.

An easy way to play and hear those altered sounds is to think of two simple things at once: the shell of the dominant chord underneath (root, 3rd, ♭7) and an ordinary major triad on top. Each triad supplies a different set of tensions.

Triads over triads: polychords

A polychord is two complete chords sounding at once, usually written one above the other. The lower triad sets the root and basic quality; the upper triad adds a block of colour. It is the same idea as the upper structures earlier in this chapter, with a full triad underneath.

Symmetrical and other scales

A few scales are built from a repeating pattern of steps, so they have no single home note and the same fingering repeats up the neck. Others are everyday scales with one note changed or added. All are specialist colours, used over particular chords.

The symmetry of diminished chords

A diminished 7 chord is four notes each a minor 3rd apart, which divides the octave into equal parts. Two things follow. Move the shape up three frets and you get the same four notes in a new order, so there are really only three different diminished 7 chords. And each one is the top four notes of four different dominant 7♭9 chords, which makes it a pivot between distant keys.

8Go deeper

Diminished patterns

The half-whole diminished scale repeats itself every three frets, so any small shape can be moved up or down in minor 3rds and stay inside the scale. Over a dominant 7♭9 chord this produces the tumbling, symmetrical runs associated with bebop and fusion.

10Checkpoint

Chapter 11 checkpoint

You are ready to move on when you can

  • Play one altered dominant resolving to its I chord.
  • Name one scale each for a maj7, m7, 7 and m7♭5 chord without looking.
  • Play one upper-structure triad over a dominant shell, resolved to I.
  • Name two places a dominant 7 chord can resolve other than down a 5th.
  • Say why there are only three different diminished 7 chords.

A ten-minute routine

  1. Two minutes: V7 to I in one key, then V7♭9 to I, then V7alt to I. Listen to the pull increase.
  2. Two minutes: one upper-structure triad over a dominant shell, resolved to I.
  3. Three minutes: play a diminished 7 chord and move it up three frets, noticing it is the same chord.
  4. Three minutes: a symmetrical scale over one chord, then the scale the reference table suggests.
