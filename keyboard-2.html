Major scale fingering

Which finger plays which note of a major scale, where the thumb tucks under, and how to practise it so it sounds even.

Why fingering matters

Fingers are numbered 1 to 5 from the thumb to the little finger, the same in both hands. A scale is longer than five notes, so at some point the hand has to move. The trick is to pass the thumb under a finger (right hand going up) or to cross a finger over the thumb (left hand going up), so the sound stays smooth.

C major is all white keys. Going up, the right hand plays 1-2-3, tucks the thumb under to play F, then continues 2-3-4-5. The left hand plays 5-4-3-2-1 up to G, then crosses finger 3 over the thumb for A and finishes 2-1. The two hands mirror each other. Coming down you use the same fingers in reverse: the right hand crosses finger 3 over the thumb, the left hand tucks the thumb under.

F major changes the right hand

F major has one black key, B♭, and finger 4 plays it. The right hand plays 1-2-3-4 for F G A B♭, then the thumb goes on C and 2-3-4 finish the scale. The left hand keeps the same 5-4-3-2-1-3-2-1 pattern as in C and G. Fingerings are conventions that make the hand comfortable, so you may see small variations in other books.

How to practise

  • Start with one octave, hands separately, and only then extend to two, three and four octaves.
  • Use a metronome at about 100 BPM, and raise it a few beats at a time once it feels even.
  • Move through the keys by adding one sharp or flat each time, in circle-of-fifths order.
  • Listen for gaps at the thumb crossing. The note after the tuck should be as loud and as long as the others.
