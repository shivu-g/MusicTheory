Reading chord charts

Follow the road map of repeats and jumps, then read chords as letters, Roman numerals or Nashville numbers.

Road map: repeats and jumps

A bar line divides the music into bars and is never played. A double bar line marks the end of a section, often before a change of key, time or tempo. A thin line plus a thick line is the final bar. Add two dots in front of it and it becomes an end-repeat: go back to the matching start-repeat (dots facing the repeated music), or to the top if there is none.

  • First and second endings: play bracket 1, repeat, then skip bracket 1 and play bracket 2.
  • D.C. (da capo) goes back to the very start. D.S. (dal segno) goes back to the segno sign.
  • al Fine means play on until the word Fine. al Coda means play on until "To Coda", then jump to the coda sign.
  • On the first pass "To Coda" is ignored. Whether repeats are taken again after a D.C. or D.S. is a convention, usually no; follow the chart or ask the bandleader.

A D.S. al Coda uses three markers: the segno (where you restart), To Coda (where you leave) and the coda sign (where you land).

Walk the map

Eight bars: a segno on bar 3, "To Coda" on bar 5, "D.S. al Coda" on bar 6, and the coda on bars 7 and 8. Step through to see the real playing order.

Order played: 1 2 3 4 5 6, jump to the segno, 3 4 5, leave at To Coda, then 7 8. Nashville and letter names are in the next section.

Chords as numbers

The Nashville number system names each chord by the scale degree of its root, with the tonic as 1. In C major, 1-4-5 means C-F-G; in B major the same numbers mean B-E-F♯. One chart works in every key, which helps when a singer asks for a different one. Everything is major until marked: minor is written with a minus sign (-6), and a 7 after the number adds a seventh (57 is G7 in C).

Roman numerals do something similar, but the case of the letters tells the quality: uppercase for major, lowercase for minor, as in I ii iii IV V vi. Symbols vary a little between writers, so ask whoever made the chart. Slash chords put numbers on both sides, so 1/3 is the tonic chord with its third in the bass.
