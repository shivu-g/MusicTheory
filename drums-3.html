Drums · D3

Rudiments

The small sticking patterns every drummer builds from: singles, doubles, triples and the paradiddle.

What a rudiment is

Rudiments are short sticking patterns, named and standardised, that train the hands to move in every combination. The international list kept by the Percussive Arts Society has 40 rudiments in four families: roll, diddle, flam and drag. Almost every fill and groove you will play is made from these pieces.

In sticking, R is the right hand and L the left. The four patterns below are the foundation:

  • Single stroke roll: R L R L. Hands strictly alternate.
  • Double stroke roll: R R L L. Two strokes per hand, so the second one is a controlled rebound.
  • Triple stroke roll: R R R L L L. Three per hand.
  • Single paradiddle: R L R R, then L R L L. Accent the first note of each group of four. The doubled stroke at the end of each group swaps the lead hand, so the pattern flips every four notes.

The diddle family also holds the double and triple paradiddle and the paradiddle-diddle, and the flam family starts with the flam, flam tap, flamacue and flam paradiddle. Those get their own pass later.

Sticking player

Rudiment
Notes per beat

The right hand sounds a higher click and the left hand a lower one, so you can hear the sticking as well as see it. Accented notes have a gold outline and a louder click. Set the tempo in the top bar and start slow.

Slow first. Evenness matters more than speed. Play each pattern at a tempo where every stroke is the same height and the same loudness, and raise the metronome only when it stays even.

Practise this

  • Five minutes: singles, then doubles, then paradiddles, eighth notes at 70 bpm on a pad.
  • Start the paradiddle with the left hand too (L R L L, R L R R) so neither hand always leads.
  • Move from eighths to sixteenths without changing the tempo, then back.
