1Core

The major scale

A scale is a set of notes chosen from the twelve. The major scale picks seven of them using a fixed pattern of steps: whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half. Start that pattern on any note and you get that note's major scale, and its key.

The major scale is also the measuring stick of music theory. Its notes are numbered 1 to 7, and every other scale or chord is described by comparison. "♭3" means a note one half step lower than the 3rd of the major scale; "♯5" means one half step higher than its 5th.

Key

W = whole step (2 frets), H = half step (1 fret). The half steps always fall between degrees 3–4 and 7–8.

Why keys have sharps or flats. In C the pattern happens to land on natural notes only. Start anywhere else and some notes must be raised or lowered to keep the pattern, and every letter must be used exactly once. That set of sharps or flats is the key signature.
2Core

The circle of fifths

The circle arranges all twelve keys so that neighbours differ by a single note. Click any key to see its signature and hear its home chord.

  • Clockwise: each key is a perfect 5th higher and gains one sharp (or loses one flat).
  • Counter-clockwise: each key is a perfect 4th higher and gains one flat.
  • Outer ring: major keys. Inner ring: the relative minor, which shares the same signature.
  • Sharps arrive in this order: F C G D A E B. Flats in the reverse: B E A D G C F.

Reading a key signature

  • Sharps: the key is one half step above the last sharp. Three sharps (F♯ C♯ G♯): last is G♯, so the key is A major.
  • Flats: the key is the second-to-last flat. Three flats (B♭ E♭ A♭): second-to-last is E♭, so the key is E♭ major. One flat is F major.
  • No sharps or flats: C major, or its relative A minor.
On the neck. Counter-clockwise is the easy direction: same fret, next thinner string (low E string to A string to D string). Clockwise is the power-chord move: next thinner string, two frets up. Both rules shift by one fret when you cross onto the B string.
3Core

Triads: stacking two thirds

A chord is three or more notes played together. The basic chord is the triad: a root, the note a 3rd above it, and the note a 5th above it. Put another way, it is two thirds stacked. There are only two sizes of third, so there are only four possible stacks.

Read each stack from the bottom up. Examples are built on C. ▶ plays the notes one by one, then together.

One note decides the mood. Major and minor differ only in the 3rd, a single fret. Find a major triad, lower its 3rd by one fret, and you have the minor triad on the same root.

Inversions. The three notes can be stacked with any of them at the bottom. Root at the bottom is root position, 3rd at the bottom is first inversion, 5th at the bottom is second inversion. It is still the same chord; only the flavour and the bass note change.

4Core

The seven triads inside a major key

Build a triad on each note of a major scale using only notes from that scale: start on a note, skip one, take one, skip one, take one. The result is the key's seven diatonic chords. The pattern of qualities is the same in every key: major, minor, minor, major, major, minor, diminished.

Key

Click a chord to hear it and to see which scale notes it uses. Roman numerals: upper case = major, lower case = minor, ° = diminished.

  • I, IV and V are the three major chords and carry a huge share of songs between them.
  • ii, iii and vi are the minor chords. vi is the relative minor: the same notes as the key, heard from a darker home base.
  • vii° is the unstable one. It contains a tritone and wants to resolve back to I.

  • Name the key signature of any key on the circle of fifths, and find its relative minor.
  • Spell the four triad types (major, minor, diminished, augmented) from any root.
  • Play a triad in root position and both inversions on one set of three strings.
  • List the seven triads of a major key with their numerals: I ii iii IV V vi vii°.

A ten-minute routine

  1. Two minutes: play a major scale in one key, saying the numbers aloud. Then move one step round the circle and repeat.
  2. Two minutes: say the sharps or flats of five keys without looking, then check on the circle.
  3. Three minutes: build the four triads on one root and play each one.
  4. Three minutes: play the seven triads of one key, saying the numerals aloud.
