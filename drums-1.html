Drums · D1

Pulse and subdivisions

How one steady beat splits into two, three or four, and how to count every split out loud.

The pulse and its splits

In 4/4 the quarter note is the pulse. You count it 1 2 3 4 and the bar repeats. Every other rhythm is a way of cutting that pulse into equal pieces.

  • Eighths cut each beat in two: 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &, eight notes per bar.
  • Sixteenths cut each beat in four: 1 e & a 2 e & a and so on, sixteen notes per bar.
  • Eighth-note triplets cut each beat in three: 1 trip let 2 trip let, twelve notes per bar. Three of them take exactly the time of one quarter note.

Silence counts too. Keep saying the whole grid through a rest, so the next note arrives on time. A dotted note lasts its own value plus half again: a dotted quarter is 1.5 beats and a dotted eighth is 0.75 of a beat.

On drum notation, notes with an x head are cymbals and round heads are drums with skins.

Subdivision counter

Split each beat in
Sound only
Press start. The metronome in the top bar sets the tempo.

The gold cell is the syllable sounding now. Choose a single syllable under "Sound only" and keep counting the rest silently: the muted cells stay in the grid.

Drill. Say the full sixteenth grid at a slow tempo and clap only on the numbers. Repeat clapping only on the "&", then only the "e", then only the "a". Your voice holds the grid while your hands pick out a part of it.

Practise this

  • Two minutes at 60 bpm per subdivision: quarters, eighths, triplets, sixteenths, counting aloud each time.
  • Switch splits every bar without letting the pulse move. The click on the beat is your anchor.
  • Play the "Sound only: &" setting and tap along on the off-beats with a foot or a pad.
