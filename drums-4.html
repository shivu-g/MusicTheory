Drums · D4

Basic grooves

Three voices, one bar: hi-hat, snare and kick. Build a groove on a grid, hear it, and change one thing at a time.

The basic rock beat

In 4/4 the standard starting groove gives each limb a job. The hi-hat keeps steady eighth notes (1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &). The snare plays on beats 2 and 4, which is called the backbeat. The kick plays on beats 1 and 3. A hi-hat or a clap can carry the backbeat on other records, but on a kit it is nearly always the snare.

Of the three voices the snare backbeat is the stable one, because it tells the listener where the bar is. The kick is the flexible voice: move it and the groove changes character while the backbeat holds.

  • Kick variation: kick on 1, the "and" of 2, and 3.
  • Four on the floor: kick on all four beats, snare still on 2 and 4.
  • Sixteenth hi-hat: the same kick and snare with the hi-hat on every sixteenth.
  • Half-time: the snare moves to beat 3 only, so the bar feels half as fast.

Change one thing. Load a preset, change a single square, and listen to what moves. Learning a groove is mostly learning which note decides its character.

Ghost notes

A ghost note is a very quiet snare note placed between the main hits. It adds texture and forward motion without competing with the backbeat. If every snare note has the same weight the groove sounds flat, so the point is contrast: ghosts barely sound, backbeats clearly do. Keep the stick low for ghosts and lift it for accents.

Where do ghosts go? In funk and shuffle patterns they typically sit on the off-beat sixteenths, the "e" and "a", around the backbeat. That is a common habit, not a rule, and the "Ghost funk" preset is one example of it. Players often find a ghost just after the backbeat easier than one leading into it.

Triplet grooves and the shuffle

Switch the grid to 12 steps and each beat splits into three, counted "1 la li". A shuffle is a long-short skipping feel built on triplets: the cymbal plays the beat and the third triplet part but skips the middle one. It can be written as triplets in 4/4 or in 12/8, which are nearly equivalent. In 12/8 the kick still sits on 1 and 3 and the snare on 2 and 4, with the hi-hat marking the three-part beat.

  • Half-time shuffle: snare on beat 3 only, a more laid-back feel.
  • Purdie-style shuffle: a half-time shuffle with soft ghost snare notes in the gaps.

Dynamic contrast matters here too: a weak difference between ghosts and accents is a common reason a shuffle sounds stiff. This page plays all triplet parts evenly; players also nudge the third part slightly later for a driving feel or slightly earlier for a lazy one, which the grid does not model.

Practise this

  • Play basic rock at 70 bpm until the hi-hat is perfectly even, then add the kick variation.
  • Say "1 e & a" aloud while you play the sixteenth hi-hat preset.
  • Try each preset with the snare removed, then put it back and notice what the backbeat adds.
