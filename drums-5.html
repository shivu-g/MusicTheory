Drums · D5

Fills

A fill is a short break from the groove that carries the music into the next section. Five starter shapes, each landing on the next downbeat.

What a fill does

A fill briefly leaves the repeating beat, usually at the end of a phrase, to link one section to the next, for example a verse into a chorus. It can last half a beat or several bars. What matters most is where it goes and how long it is, not how busy it is. Leaving space often works better than filling every gap.

A fill always ends by landing on the next downbeat, normally with a crash cymbal and the kick on beat 1 of the new section. If the fill is lost the groove lands, so practise the landing as much as the fill.

Five starter shapes

All of these are played in the last bar after three bars of basic rock. Notes are counted on the sixteenth or triplet grid you met in D1 and D4.

  • Eighths around the toms: two notes each on snare, high tom, mid tom and floor tom, one drum per half beat, moving down the kit.
  • Sixteenths, one drum per beat: the same order with four notes on each drum.
  • Crescendo: eight eighths on the snare, getting louder across the bar.
  • Triplets in the Bonham style: on each beat, high tom on the beat, floor tom on "trip", kick on "let", four beats in a row.
  • Linear sixteenths: right, left, right, kick, repeated. No two limbs play together, so every note is a single stroke.

Fill player

Fill
Bar1 of 4

The loop plays three bars of basic rock and then the fill, with a crash and kick on the first beat of the next loop. Each note shows its sticking (R, L, or K for kick) and drum (sn snare, t1 high tom, t2 mid tom, ft floor tom, k kick). Alternating hands is a common choice here, not the only one; try leading with your weaker hand too.

Flams. A flam is a very light grace note just before the main stroke. It fattens one note of a fill, and it is one of the rudiments still to come. Practise every fill slowly with a click first.

Practise this

  • Loop three bars of groove and one bar of fill at 60 bpm, and make the crash land exactly with the kick.
  • Play each fill with the left hand leading, then the right.
  • Play one fill, then deliberately play a bar with no fill and hear how the next section feels different.
