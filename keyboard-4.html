Seventh chords and rootless voicings

Four-note left-hand voicings that leave out the root, and how alternating two types carries you smoothly through a ii-V-I.

Leave the root out

When a bass player (or your other hand) already plays the root, a chord does not need it again. A rootless voicing is a four-note chord that skips the root and stacks the colour notes: the 3rd, 5th, 7th and 9th. There are two standard orders.

  • Type A has the 3rd at the bottom: 3-5-7-9.
  • Type B has the 7th at the bottom: 7-9-3-5.

The quality of the chord changes which notes are flat. A minor 9 chord in type A is ♭3-5-♭7-9. A dominant 7♭9 chord in type A is 3-5-♭7-♭9, and in type B it is ♭7-♭9-3-5. Teachers vary on the details: some drop the 5th of a dominant chord to leave 3-7-9. Use the spelling here as a starting point, not the only answer.

The big benefit: if you alternate type A and type B through a ii-V-I, each chord is only a step or two from the last, so the hand barely moves.
