Short ii – V – I cells

In faster tunes the ii and V chords last only two beats each, so the whole ii – V fits in one bar. That is four eighth notes per chord: just enough to state the chord and aim at the next one. Learn a few cells like these in every key and you have ready-made vocabulary for most jazz standards.

Key
Cell

Dark chips start each chord. Red chips are notes from outside the key. The tablature shows one way to finger it; numbers are frets and the top line is the high E string.

3Core

Patterns around the cycle

A classic way to learn every key is to take one short pattern and play it on each chord around the cycle of fifths, where every root is a 4th above the last. Twelve chords later you are back where you started and have played the pattern in all twelve keys.

Dominant chords that resolve elsewhere

A dominant 7 chord usually falls a 5th to its target, but it has other exits. Knowing them tells you what a surprising chord in a tune is doing, and which scale suits it: a dominant heading down a 5th takes Mixolydian or the altered scale; a dominant going anywhere else usually sounds best with Lydian dominant.

Diminished patterns

The half-whole diminished scale repeats itself every three frets, so any small shape can be moved up or down in minor 3rds and stay inside the scale. Over a dominant 7♭9 chord this produces the tumbling, symmetrical runs associated with bebop and fusion.

Mode studies

A short study for any mode. Each one is built the same way: outline the home chord, then keep returning to the mode's characteristic note so that its colour is unmistakable. Play it slowly over a held bass note on the root.

Root

Red chips are the characteristic note.

7Core

Walking bass lines

A walking bass plays one note on every beat and connects each chord to the next. A reliable recipe for a bar with one chord: root, a step up, another chord tone, then a note a half step away from the next root. On guitar, playing these on the bottom strings is the first step toward accompanying yourself with bass and chords together.

Form

Dark chips are roots; red chips are half-step approach notes leading to the next root.

Chapter 13 checkpoint

You have completed the workshop when you can

  • Play two short ii – V – I cells in three keys from memory.
  • Play one pattern around the whole cycle without stopping.
  • Name two places a dominant 7 chord can resolve other than down a 5th.
  • Walk a bass line through a 12-bar blues.

A ten-minute routine

  1. Three minutes: one ii – V – I cell, moved through four keys.
  2. Three minutes: one pattern around the cycle with the metronome.
  3. Four minutes: a walking bass line through the blues, then improvise your own using the recipe.
