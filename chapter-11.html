The voicing builder

Chapter 5 introduced drop 2 and drop 3. They belong to a family. Start with a seventh chord stacked in thirds (close position), number its notes from the top, and drop one or two of them an octave. Each recipe spreads the chord differently, and each one gives four inversions.

Chords with open strings

Letting open strings ring against fretted notes higher up the neck gives a shimmering sound that no barre chord can produce. There is no fixed system for these, so this finder searches for them: it looks for shapes with the root in the bass where every open string used is a note of the chord.

Chord

Small dots to the left of the nut are open strings. Keys with sharps that match the open strings (E, A, D, G) give the most results.

Fourth voicings from any scale

The fourth voicings of chapter 5 can be built from any seven-note scale and with more notes. Three-note stacks are light and mobile; four-note stacks are fuller. Melodic and harmonic minor produce more stacks with a tritone or an unusual gap, which is the source of their darker colour.

Triads over triads

A polychord is two complete chords sounding at once, usually written one above the other. The lower triad sets the root and basic quality; the upper triad adds a block of colour. It is the same idea as the upper structures of chapter 8, with a full triad underneath.

The symmetry of diminished chords

A diminished 7 chord is four notes each a minor 3rd apart, which divides the octave into equal parts. Two things follow. Move the shape up three frets and you get the same four notes in a new order, so there are really only three different diminished 7 chords. And each one is the top four notes of four different dominant 7♭9 chords, which makes it a pivot between distant keys.

You are ready to move on when you can

  • Explain how a drop 2 and a drop 3 voicing are made from a close-position chord.
  • Play one seventh chord through four inversions on one string group.
  • Find one open-string voicing for a chord in the key of E, A or D.
  • Say why there are only three different diminished 7 chords.

A ten-minute routine

  1. Four minutes: one chord type, four inversions, up and down one string group.
  2. Three minutes: a ii – V – I using the nearest inversion each time.
  3. Three minutes: one open-string voicing, used in place of the plain chord in a song you know.
