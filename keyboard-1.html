Keyboard · K1

Keys and layout

Where the 88 keys sit, how octave numbers count, and how the black-key groups let you find any note without counting from the left.

The pattern that never changes

A full-size piano has 88 keys: 52 white and 36 black. They run from A0 at the far left to C8 at the far right, which is seven complete octaves plus three extra notes at the bottom end. The twelve-note pattern of the keys (seven white, five black) simply repeats.

The black keys come in a group of two and a group of three. Those groups are your map. The white key just to the left of every two-black-key group is C. The white key just left of every three-black-key group is F. From any C, the white keys climb C D E F G A B and then you reach the next C.

Octave numbers change at C, not at A. So the key after B3 is C4. Middle C is C4, the fourth C from the left and key number 40, sounding at roughly 261.6 Hz. Keys are numbered 1 to 88 from the left, so A0 is key 1 and C8 is key 88.
